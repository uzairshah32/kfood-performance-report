Part 1Financials & economics

Executive summary

Over the eight months to July 2026, KFood generated Rs 2.48M in landed (gross) sales and Rs 2.03M in delivered (net) sales, a healthy 82% GMV realisation. The March rebrand widened the range from ghee-only to ghee plus flour, which grew order volume but pulled average order value down from Rs 5,299 to Rs 2,430. Underlying product margins are strong (~60% gross), ghee is the profit and acquisition engine, and repeat purchasing is building fast (returning-customer rate up from 3% to 27%). The business is deliberately investing ahead of profit, and the Rs 500k/month content engine launching in July is the lever meant to lift blended ROAS from ~2.1 toward the 3.5+ target.

The content plan

Content has been the underfunded engine. The new Rs 500k/month plan is built to change that.

Until now, content ran on roughly Rs 20–30k/month, with only two real spikes (Rs 244k in Dec, Rs 200k in April). At that level there was no creative velocity, no consistent voice, and stale ad creative — which is exactly why ROAS has sat around 2. The step up to Rs 500k/month from July is a deliberate, one-time reset of the engine, and the reason we are confident in this plan is that the prior under-investment leaves obvious, cheap wins on the table.

Phase 1 · Days 0–30 — Foundation & velocity

ROAS target: hold 1.7–2.0

Onboard execution, build the ghee + flour asset library, seed 5–10 barter food influencers, test 8–12 creative angles. Identify the top 3 angles.

Phase 2 · Days 31–60 — Optimization & lift

ROAS target: 2.5–3.0

Cut bottom-quartile creative, lock 2–3 food influencers on retainer, shift spend to winning angles, tighten brand voice.

Phase 3 · Days 61–90 — Scale & lock the playbook

ROAS target: 3.5+

Scale proven creative-SKU-audience combinations, lock 3–4 ongoing influencer partnerships, document the playbook for sustained scale.

A more cohesive approach going forward

Treat ecom + socials as a channel, not a separate entity.

The biggest structural unlock is not a metric — it is how the ecom and socials function plugs into the rest of KFood. Today it operates somewhat downstream. Going forward it should be treated as a core channel that is looped in early, so it can plan properly rather than react.

  • Loop us in on every new launch. When a new product or initiative is coming, the socials side can plan content around it in advance, and the ecom side can assess early whether it makes sense to sell online at all — before it ships, not after.
  • Plan ecom deliberately, product by product. Not everything belongs on the DTC store. Some SKUs are volume plays for retail; others (like the ghee bundle) are built for online. Deciding this upfront keeps the store focused on what actually converts and contributes.
  • Then build the content moat on what only KFood can say. With the 500k engine as the base, the content north star should hammer the things competitors can’t copy: farmer stories, traceability, quality of inputs, and the farm-to-table system. That is the durable differentiator behind the PCSIR, GMP and Halal badges, and it is what turns a ghee ad into a brand rather than a commodity offer.
Achha and Dry Delights can outspend KFood on ad volume and match a “flat 10% off” offer. What none of them can easily match is a credible, farmer-powered supply story told consistently. That story, produced at velocity through the content engine, is how KFood earns a premium instead of competing on price.
